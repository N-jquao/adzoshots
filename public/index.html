The city keeps a second shift.

I photograph Accra between ten at night and six in the morning — the fish landings, the ring road, the market women setting up under bare bulbs. Nine years, mostly on film, mostly by available light.

Frame 04/36

Landing

2019 — ongoing · Jamestown, Accra

The canoes come in around four. Before that there is nothing to see and everything to hear — two hundred people on a beach in the dark, arranging themselves. I started shooting this because I couldn't sleep. I kept going because nobody had photographed the waiting, only the fish.

f/2 · 1/15 · ISO 3200 · Delta 3200 pushed to 6400

Frame 09/36

Sodium

2021 — 2023 · James Town Post Office

Accra replaced its sodium vapour streetlights with LEDs over eighteen months. Sodium light is orange and forgiving and makes everyone look like they belong to the same evening. LED is white and correct and tells the truth. I shot the last of the orange before it went. This series is an archive of a colour.

Frame 15/36

Second Shift

2020 — ongoing · Accra

Portraits of people working while the city sleeps. A baker in Nima who starts at one. A security guard at a bank who has read every book in the branch. Two nurses who share a thermos. Shot on a tripod at 1/4 of a second, so everyone had to hold still for a moment they'd otherwise have spent alone.

Frame 22/36

Harmattan Nights

December 2022 · Accra, Kumasi

For six weeks a year, dust off the Sahara turns every light source into a soft box. Streetlamps get haloes. Headlights become volumes you can see the shape of. I shot only in December, only outdoors, and did almost nothing in the darkroom. The air did the work.

Frame 29/36

Kaneshie, 03:00

2023 · Kaneshie Market, Accra

The market builds itself every night before it opens. Crates, tarpaulins, one bulb per stall, phone torches held in teeth. It is finished by six and looks permanent by seven. Nobody sees the assembly. This is the assembly.

About

I'm Adzo. I've been photographing at night for nine years, which is a strange thing to build a life around and I don't fully recommend it.

I came to this backwards. I trained as a sound engineer and spent four years mixing live shows, which meant I was awake at three in the morning anyway, walking home through a city that looked nothing like the one I'd left in the afternoon. I borrowed a camera to try to explain that to someone. I never gave it back.

I shoot mostly medium format film — a Mamiya RZ67 that weighs more than it should, Portra 800 and Delta 3200 pushed hard. Available light only. No flash, ever. If the light isn't there, the picture isn't there, and I go home.

I print my own work in a converted bathroom in Osu, selenium-toned on fibre paper. The toning is why the blacks in my prints lean slightly purple, which people sometimes think is a filter. It is a chemical.

My work has been shown at Nubuke Foundation and the Accra Photo Festival, and has run in Guardian Weekend, Le Monde, and the Financial Times. In 2024 I was a finalist for the CAP Prize.

I take a small number of commissions each year. If your project happens after dark, we'll get on.

Process

01 · Call

Twenty minutes, no charge. You tell me what the project is. I tell you if I'm the right person, and if I'm not, I'll name someone who is.

02 · Scout

For anything on location I visit first, usually at the hour we'd shoot. Night light changes block by block. This is not optional and it is not billed extra.

03 · Shoot

Film gets developed the next morning. You'll see a contact sheet within 72 hours — the full roll, unflattering frames included.

04 · Deliver

Edited scans over WeTransfer, plus a printed contact sheet in the post. Prints, if ordered, follow in four weeks.

Questions

Do you use flash?

No. If a project needs lighting, I'm the wrong photographer and I'll say so on the first call.

Film or digital?

Film for anything with a runway. Digital when the deadline is same-day. I'll tell you which before we start, not after.

How long until I see pictures?

Contact sheet within 72 hours. Edited scans in ten days. Darkroom prints in four weeks.

Do you travel?

Yes, anywhere. Travel and accommodation are billed at cost with receipts.

Can I buy a print of a commissioned image?

Yes, if the commissioning client agrees. Ask and I'll ask them.

Do you shoot weddings?

Occasionally, and only the reception — the part that happens after dark. I don't do getting-ready, first looks, or group formals. There are people much better at that than me.

Get in touch

The more you tell me now, the more useful my reply is. Dates and rough budget especially — I'd rather say no quickly than waste a fortnight of yours.
